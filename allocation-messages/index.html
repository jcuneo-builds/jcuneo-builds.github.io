Monthly Allocation Messages

Two n8n workflows that make resourcing transparent: every person learns where their time goes next month, and every client lead sees how their team is changing before it happens.

The problem

Resourcing decisions nobody heard about

Operations planned each person's monthly split across clients, but the plan sat in a database most people never opened. Teams and client leads were left working from guesswork.

Before

No view of your own allocations Guessing how to split your week Team changes discovered late Reactive client management

People didn't know how their time was meant to be spent, so they couldn't plan it. Client leads had no visibility into who was joining or leaving their teams, because nothing connected resourcing to the people running the account.

After

Your split, in Slack Team changes before the month starts Client, non-client and open time Proactive client handovers

On the last working day, each person gets a clear breakdown of next month and each client lead gets the people moving on their accounts, with time to brief the client and plan handovers.

Why it mattered: a client lead who learns about a team change on day one of the month is already behind. Unannounced changes erode client trust, and the fix is a heads-up at the right moment, not more meetings.

The solution

The right data, to the right person, on the right day

Two scheduled n8n workflows wake every weekday and exit cleanly unless it's the send day. On the send day, one resolves every NAMER team member across three systems and DMs them their split. The other diffs this month against next month for every client a lead owns and DMs them exactly who is joining and leaving.

Architecture

Three systems, two workflows, one inbox

Two n8n workflows on their own schedules read the same allocation export and share one engine for send-day logic, identity matching and safety checks.

Engineering highlights

Small build, careful engineering

Identity resolution with no shared key

The allocation export has names but no emails, the people database has emails under slightly different names, and Slack only accepts an email. Names are normalized (accents, case, spacing), known mismatches have overrides, and a loose match is accepted only when exactly one person fits. Anyone unmatched is reported, never guessed.

A send date the code works out itself

The schedule fires daily and a code step computes the last US working day, building that year's federal holidays (observed dates, Thanksgiving, Labor Day and the rest) from the calendar. Override dates handle one-off early sends without double-sending.

Fail-closed by design

Test mode reroutes every DM to the owner with a note of who it was for. An allow-list throws before anything sends if the recipient count is off, and a schema check stops the run if a sheet column is renamed. Changes ship against live data with no risk to real inboxes.

Rate-aware and self-reporting

Slack lookups run in batches of 15 with a 20-second pause to stay under rate limits. Sends retry, then continue on error, and every run ends with a report listing sends, failures and unmatched people, so issues surface without opening n8n.

Outcome

What changed

Proactive

Client leads see team changes before the month starts

Live since the 30 October send. Leads now get time to brief clients, plan handovers and protect the relationship, instead of explaining a change after the fact.

Transparent

Everyone knows where their time should go

A plain breakdown of client, non-client and open time turns an opaque plan into something people can act on when they plan their week.

Hands-off

No recurring ops chore

No exports, no drafted messages, no calendar reminders. The workflows pick the day, resolve the people and report back.

Observable

A data-quality check for free

Every unmatched name or missing email is flagged after each run, which keeps the people data honest month to month.

Reusable

A template for Slack automation

The send-day gate, identity matching and test harness drop straight into the next workflow that needs to message people.
